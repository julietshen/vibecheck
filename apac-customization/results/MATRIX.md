# Results matrix — open safety models × APAC languages

All runs reproduce from this folder (see per-model runners). Metric = **F1** on the
harm-in-that-language under one generic plain-language policy. **Steerability** = % of
flagged items released when the policy is rewritten to *permit* the harm (a true
policy-following model releases ~all; a fixed-prior model keeps flagging). Small,
single-labeler samples — directional, not definitive. Bengali is romanized/code-mixed
Bangla (TB-OLID); Chinese is ChiFraud; SEA is the synthetic moneylending set.

## What the matrix says

1. **The gap is the language, not the model type.** cope-b is *both* 100% steerable *and*
   the best multilingual all-rounder. The steerable-vs-multilingual trade-off I first
   hypothesized is too strong — the robust failure is **romanized/low-resource Bengali**,
   where every model drops.
2. **"Multilingual" is non-uniform.** Shieldstral is best on SEA (1.00) and Chinese
   (0.946) yet **worst on romanized Bengali (0.387, recall 0.27)** — code-mixed romanized
   script is out of its distribution. Always test the *exact* languages you serve.
3. **Fine-tuning is not a guaranteed fix.** LoRA fixed the objective harm (moneylending:
   SEA 0.857→1.00, Chinese 0.769→0.880) but **hurt** the subjective harm in the hardest
   language (Bengali offensive 0.643→0.308 — recall collapsed to 0.20). 270 examples on a
   1.7B model taught it to under-flag. Fine-tuning needs enough good data, and subjective
   culturally-loaded harms in low-resource languages are genuinely hard.
4. **Only one model reasons in-language** (safeguard) — a real edge for auditability, at
   the highest cost/latency and the lowest Chinese recall.

## The honest takeaways for the talk

- No single open model gives you steerable + multilingual + auditable + cheap. You compose.
- The romanized / low-resource language cliff is where open safety tooling fails for APAC —
  and it falls hardest on exactly the languages under-represented in the tooling's training.
- Customization (fine-tune) helps for clear harms with data; it is not a substitute for
  language coverage or good labels. Sometimes the answer is "collect better regional data,"
  which is a job for the practitioners in the room.
